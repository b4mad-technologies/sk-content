A hippopotamus basks in muddy shallows with the stillness of a river rock, then erupts into motion when its territory is disturbed. Its broad jaws and crushing bulk make even narrow waterways feel perilous, especially where reeds conceal a submerged path. Herds are noisy, protective, and quick to turn a peaceful ford into a panicked retreat.
